02 · Method

We coded 137 optimizer papers from full text and checked the coding blind.

Purposive sample of 2022–26 papers across 15 technique layers; every objective needs a verbatim design quote.
1

Discovery

6 search agents · 210 unique papers
2

Selection (purposive)

  • 137 optimizers read in full (133 + 4 added after review)
  • 10 metric/benchmark papers
  • 55 abstract-only, kept separate (of 60; 5 unclear)
  • 7 excluded
3

Extraction

16 agents · one JSON record per paper · re-pass demoted ~48 headline-only entries
4

Blind checks

  • re-extraction of 30 papers: primary objective 28/30, Jaccard 0.74, exact sets 14/30
  • tail tiers recoded blind: 25/30, κ = 0.76
  • tail audit of 40 papers
  • 181 definition quotes verified

Analysis

16 metric families · Wilson 95 % CIs · scripted counts
five adversarial review rounds of the synthesis (R1–R5)

03 · Definitions

On one request's timeline, TPOT averages the gaps that TBT counts one by one.

TTFT = t₁ − a, counted from arrival, so it includes queueing; E2E = tₙ − a = TTFT + (n − 1)·TPOT.

TPOT: one value per request, the mean gap. Its P99 is a tail over requests.

TBT (each gap; vLLM calls it ITL): every gap is one sample. Its P99 is a tail over tokens and sees stalls.

[Syn] notation: DEFINITIONS §0, SYNTHESIS v6.1 §2 · [P] Etalon arxiv_2407.07000 §2.2 (TTFT includes scheduling delay); MLPerf LoadGen TPOT = (latency − TTFT)/(n − 1), INDUSTRY_DEFS §1 · schematic, not to scale

06 · The constraint sets the number

Tightening the TPOT bound from 50 to 15 ms cuts CloudMatrix384’s decode throughput 3.6-fold.

DeepSeek-R1 decode on CloudMatrix-Infer, 4,096-token prompts, 256 output tokens: measured tokens/s per NPU at three TPOT bounds.
3.6× = 1,943 / 538, derived

A throughput number means little without its latency bound. At 15 ms the paper runs batch 8 per NPU instead of 96, so each NPU emits fewer tokens per second. Throughput-only comparisons hide this trade-off.

08 · Load regime

60 of 137 papers never evaluate under online arrivals; their results exclude queueing.

That is 44 % of full-text optimizers. Load class by technique layer: online arrivals, mixed, offline batch, single sequence, analytical.

Load class coded per paper in the post-review re-pass; "mixed" = main claims include an online component plus others; four papers first coded mixed (DeepSpeed-Inference, SGLang, EAGLE-3, KVFlow) were recoded offline-batch.

All quantization and KV-compression papers (14 of 14) and 10 of 13 speculative-decoding papers report single-sequence or offline-batch results only. Their speed-ups exclude queueing, so they do not compare with schedulers' latencies.

10 · Metrics over time

TTFT and TPOT/TBT targeting rose to ~45 % of our papers; E2E and throughput stayed flat.

Share of papers whose method targets each metric family (objective or constraint), by first-arXiv period; 137 full-text optimizers (34 / 57 / 46 papers).
Targeting = objective or constraint. TPOT/TBT rose as a constraint (3 % → 37 %), not as an objective (0 % → 7 %); goodput/SLO attainment is the only objective whose rise survives Holm (6 % → 30 %, Holm p = 0.03). The TTFT and TPOT/TBT rises also appear with abstract-coded papers added (8 % → 30 %, 3 % → 30 %) and point the same way within online- or mixed-load papers (unadjusted).

SLO framing itself is flat here (s11); the named metrics changed.

Why the falls are confounded by corpus mix

Model-, kernel- and parallelism-layer papers were 56 % → 37 % → 17 % of the set (no 2025–26 compression paper read in full). In the other 89 papers the falls shrink and lose significance (memory 27 % → 13 %, quality 27 % → 8 %, single-sequence speed 7 % → 5 %, all n.s.); TTFT still rises 13 % → 55 % (Holm p = 0.04).

13 · Stalls

Etalon's capacity ranking flips with the metric: Sarathi-Serve leads on TPOT, trails on P99 TBT.

Highest load (QPS) meeting each criterion at a 25 ms target; Llama-3-8B on H100, Arxiv-Summarization requests (Etalon Fig. 6a).
Bar heights read from the rendered figure against gridlines, ±0.02 QPS (two blind reads agree within 0.01 QPS); paper text gives "2x lower" P99-TBT capacity for Sarathi-Serve and "same at 0.6 QPS" by fluidity. One model (Llama3-8B), one H100, one dataset.

A per-request mean TPOT averages stalls away; P99 TBT counts them; Etalon's fluidity index tracks per-token deadlines and rates both systems at about 0.6 QPS. The metric, not only the system, decides which design looks better.

14 · Same name, different quantity

One metric name, several quantities: TTFT, goodput and throughput each have rival definitions.

Papers assigned to each definition variant among the 143 full-text records available when the taxonomy was built; a paper can fall into several variants.

Variant assignments listed paper by paper in analysis/def_variants.py; counts are papers and are not mutually exclusive.

Benchmark harnesses agree that TTFT includes server-side queueing, and on TPOT, but differ on ITL, on which tokens throughput counts, and on goodput; MLPerf gates TTFT and TPOT each at P99; 11 of 13 max-rate (GP1) goodput papers use 90 %.
